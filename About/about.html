About this Space



My intention for this website is to make users feel a loss of authorship and prompt them to realize that they should reclaim control of the web. Their contributions are either rewritten or erased, because I want to focus on exposing the system (large corporate power controlling the web), the system that failed the user agency.

How Should Users Interact?

This website begins with an instagram post that asks users to share it or not, but whichever button they choose, the post shows that it has already been shared on the next webpage. This shows that all individuals want to establish visibility online or wish to be seen, but our contribution under a system is not co-authorship.

On the next page, there is a game. The game involves clicking silkscreens to try to change it from replicated copies made by AI or systems back to being its original versions. The replicate copies came from users’ shared data online, which alludes to how user creativity ends up serving larger systems (e.g. uses it to train their AI). The original version of silkscreens, is a reference back to the instagram account at the beginning.

The game also has a counter on the top right, which shows how many silkscreens have been converted back to originals, and yet shows the users that although they clicked on some/all, the system still consumed all of it, since there is a large computer screen below the entire time consuming.

The next page shows something else the system feeds on, natural resources. The locusts devoured the land, just like how systems drained earth’s resources. The locusts is a reference from the popular bible story the Ten Plagues of Egypt, and locusts not only references biblical suffering and environmental harm, but also alludes to the experience of users online, left with no agency or privacy. Technology exhausts humans and the environment. When the user reads a bible line to the system, it rewrites their words. When users choose between buttons as if making a decision, both buttons lead them to the same result. Moreover, the importance of the religious references present in the silkscreen cathedral prints and the locusts plague, lies in how it explores how users are under surveillance of systems and religion, and we all have a desire “to be protected, watched, known”. This concept is inspired by Chia Amisola’s “Cathedral” video performance.

Furthermore, an additional cyanotype page features a centered cyanotype, allowing users to customize it with flowers/plants. The center print already has robotic machinery images, symbolising how nature is surrounded by technology, while plants left a white space and disappeared. The question below the cyanotype intended to show how human effort is transformed or replaced by technology in the art-making process. Avant-garde artists in the 1920s embraced new technologies to make photograms (or silkscreens). But in today’s world, we fully embraced technology, raising questions about originality.

At the end, there is a collaborative activity, prompting users to reflect on the impact of systems: how resources get depleted, and how artists change creative roles and shape meaning in art.
